SAP is not replaced. It is extended.

SAP stays the system of record for finance, procurement, inventory and orders.

Around it, small purpose-built apps reach the people and processes SAP was never designed to serve economically.

The long tail outside the ERP

The last mile

No SAP access on the floor or in the field.

Re-keying

Paper typed in at shift end.

Approvals in chats

Slow, with no record of who approved what.

Shadow IT

Fragile macros and spreadsheets holding critical data.

Slow change

Every tweak becomes a change request.

Four rules keep it clean

The ERP stays close to standard. The edge stays governed.

  • One master, one owner: SAP owns every master record
  • Read freely, write deliberately: only approved records go back
  • No custom code inside SAP
  • Business-owned, IT-governed

Six integration rules

Applied to every interface in the pattern.

  • A data contract per interface
  • Validation before write-back
  • A unique reference so retries cannot post twice
  • Failures go to a visible queue with alerts
  • Every exchange logged on both sides
  • ERP upgrades change the layer, not every app

What it aims for

Delivered / observed

    Planned / projected

    • A working demo of a first process in about a week
    • A typical app at production quality in 2 to 3 weeks
    • Rule or form changes in days, not change requests

    These are target delivery timings for the approach, not measured client results.

    Closing the edge gaps comes before AI

    AI needs structured, timely data and clear processes. Phone calls and notebooks give it nothing to work with.
