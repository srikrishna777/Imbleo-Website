The software was there. The alignment was not.

The supplier sells premium facade materials into a specification-led market: architects specify, builders buy.

Zoho was already in place, but had grown messy: unused modules, misrouted forms, broken workflows.

What the audit found

Too many doors

Forms, calls, lead providers, chat and tickets, each handled differently.

Sales run as support

New business was often worked as support tickets.

Samples by phone

Requesting a sample meant calling or filling a generic form.

Tool switching

Support history lived in Desk, deals in CRM.

Next stage: scoped, not yet claimed

These were designed or piloted. Each keeps a person in the loop.

  • AI lead-research dossiers, written back to CRM
  • Branded specification documents from CRM and product data
  • Supplier spreadsheet to customer quote, with a review gate
  • Quote timestamps and turnaround dashboards
  • Payment schedules that draft milestone invoices for finance

What changed

Delivered / observed

  • CRM became the main workspace for enquiries and sales
  • A custom sample-order journey connected directly to CRM
  • Structured intake and routing across sources and stakeholder types
  • Support-ticket context inside CRM
  • A cleaner, audited configuration with simpler views and buttons

Business-case ROI estimates exist but were not measured after go-live, so none are shown.

Audit before you add

More features on a weak setup make things worse. Fix routing, data and roles first, then build.
