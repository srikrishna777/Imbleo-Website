04 / THE INTERACTIVE EXPERIENCE · BUSINESS ANATOMY

Look under
the surface.

Peel back the layers of a business. Follow the customer journey. See where the connections work and where they break.

Meet Leah, a site manager for a building firm and a customer of Corbel Building Supply: an illustrative construction products and technical supply business. Scroll to follow one order through every team, hand-off and system behind it.

Scroll to begin
01 / 10Above the surface
01 · Above the surface

Leah sees one supplier.

She downloaded a spec sheet, asked a technical question, approved a quote and took a delivery on site. Most customers only ever see the outside of your business.

02 · The customer journey

Six moments. One order.

A spec sheet. A technical question. A quote. A site delivery. An invoice. A repeat order. Follow Leah from her first download to the next project she brings you.

03 · Peel back the first layer

One order. Six teams making it happen.

Tom in trade sales, Nadia on the technical desk, Raj in the warehouse, Sam running deliveries, Grace in accounts and Ola in customer care. Leah never meets most of them.

04 · The handovers

The work is only as good as the handover.

Follow the joins between teams, not just the tasks inside them. A dashed line is a person carrying context by email, phone or memory.

05 · The layer underneath

The technology sits underneath the work.

An inbox, a spreadsheet, a CRM nobody fully trusts, a stock list, the accounting system and a phone line. Each one useful. None of them talking.

06 · Where the thread breaks

The gaps are where the business does work twice.

Waiting. Chasing. Re-typing. Relying on somebody’s memory. These scenarios are illustrative, but most teams recognise at least one.

  • The spec request is lost in the inbox
  • Quantities are re-typed for the warehouse
  • The site extra never reaches the invoice
  • Leah explains her project again
07 · Look closer · Technical → Warehouse

An approved quote should not wait for a conversation.

Nadia has the specs, the quantities and the site details. Raj still has to rebuild them from emails before Sam’s truck can be loaded.

08 · Fix one handover

The approval creates the order.

When Leah approves the quote, the order is created with the specs, quantities and delivery address attached, and Raj is notified. Same people. Less carrying.

09 · Back to the business

The same business. Fewer gaps to carry.

Close the loudest gap first, then improve each handover one at a time. Ownership stays clear the whole way.

10 · Monday, 8:00am

One screen. Nobody had to prepare it.

Better visibility comes from recording the work properly in the first place. The numbers arrive because people simply did their jobs.

THE END OF THE THREAD

The apps aren’t the point. The thread is.

Pick one customer you supplied last month. Can you follow the whole thread, from their first spec request to their next order, without opening another system or ringing a colleague?
