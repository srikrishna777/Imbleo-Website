04 / THE INTERACTIVE EXPERIENCE · BUSINESS ANATOMY

Look under
the surface.

Peel back the layers of a business. Follow the customer journey. See where the connections work and where they break.

Meet Leah, a customer of Brightwater Kitchens: an illustrative kitchen renovation business. Scroll to follow one customer through every team, hand-off and system behind her new kitchen.

Scroll to begin
01 / 10Above the surface
01 · Above the surface

Leah sees one business.

She saw an ad, visited a showroom, signed a quote and got a new kitchen. Most customers only ever see the outside of your business.

02 · The customer journey

Six moments. One story.

An ad. A showroom chat. A signed quote. Install day. An invoice. A friend she refers. Follow Leah from her first click to the neighbour she sends your way.

03 · Peel back the first layer

One kitchen. Six teams making it happen.

Tom in marketing, Nadia in sales, Raj in operations, Sam rostering the crews, Grace in finance and Ola in customer care. Leah never meets most of them.

04 · The handovers

The work is only as good as the handover.

Follow the joins between teams, not just the tasks inside them. A dashed line is a person carrying context by email, phone or memory.

05 · The layer underneath

The technology sits underneath the work.

An inbox, a spreadsheet, a CRM nobody fully trusts, a job board, the accounting system and a phone line. Each one useful. None of them talking.

06 · Where the thread breaks

The gaps are where the business does work twice.

Waiting. Chasing. Re-typing. Relying on somebody’s memory. These scenarios are illustrative, but most teams recognise at least one.

  • The ad source is lost in the inbox
  • Measurements are re-typed for the job
  • The variation never reaches the invoice
  • Leah explains her kitchen again
07 · Look closer · Sales → Operations

A signed quote should not wait for a conversation.

Nadia has the measurements, the photos and the agreed design. Raj still has to rebuild them from emails before he can book Ben’s crew.

08 · Fix one handover

The signature creates the job.

When Leah signs, the job is created with the measurements, photos and design attached, and Raj is notified. Same people. Less carrying.

09 · Back to the business

The same business. Fewer gaps to carry.

Close the loudest gap first, then improve each handover one at a time. Ownership stays clear the whole way.

10 · Monday, 8:00am

One screen. Nobody had to prepare it.

Better visibility comes from recording the work properly in the first place. The numbers arrive because people simply did their jobs.

THE END OF THE THREAD

The apps aren’t the point. The thread is.

Pick one customer you delivered for last month. Can you follow the whole thread, from first enquiry to the friend they referred, without opening another system or ringing a colleague?
